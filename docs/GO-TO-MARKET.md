# What has to happen before this beats Mavek

Written 20 September 2026, the day after `COMPARISON-MAVEK.md`. That document
ended on a verdict: a better engine inside a product that does not exist yet,
against a real product with a thinner engine and a marketing surface that has
outrun it. This document is the work list that changes the second half of that
sentence without spending the first half.

Read `docs/opinion/001-2026-09-16.md` before this one. Nothing here overrides
it. The positioning recommendation there still holds: sell the audit, grow into
the retainer claim.

---

## The finding that sets the order of everything below

Our own engine, run against our own live site on 20 September 2026:

```
https://fullseoaeogeotool.jishhnnuu.workers.dev/
8 pages · 42 findings · health 84.0 · aeo 51.5
```

Against Mavek's site the day before:

```
https://mavek.ai/
40 pages · 120 findings · health 80.3 · aeo 62.6
```

**Mavek's site scores eleven points higher on AI answer readiness than ours
does.** They publish an llms.txt; we return 404. They ship Organization,
Breadcrumb and Article JSON-LD on every page; we ship none. They name every AI
crawler in robots.txt and allow it explicitly; we serve Cloudflare's default
content-signals file, which contains no directives at all. They have a
sitemap with 141 URLs; we have no sitemap and eight pages.

## Phase 0. Earn the right to be believed

**Target: one week. Nothing here needs money, a company, or a decision.**

### 0.1 Fix the three defects in our own engine

From `COMPARISON-MAVEK.md` section 9. In priority order:

1. `schema_contradicts_page` fires site-wide on a legitimate `Organization`
   description. Forty high-severity false positives on forty pages of Mavek's
   site. Narrow the Python check to what the TypeScript one does, or narrow
   both to a rule that cannot fire on a site-wide block.
2. The two engines disagree on that code, which CLAUDE.md forbids.
3. `ScoreBreakdown` in `analysis/scoring.py` has no `measured` flag, so the
   CLI printed "authority 100.0" for a site with zero link data. Invariant 2
   holds on the Worker and fails on the server install.

Then reconcile the catalogues: 90 TypeScript, 74 Python, 22 codes in one
engine only, 6 in the other, and a README banner that says 71.

**Why first:** the pitch is "a false positive is more expensive than a miss".
A demo that produces forty of them on the prospect's own site ends the call.

### 0.2 Pass our own checks

Take the twelve codes above and ship the fixes. The engine generates most of
them already, which is the demo:

- `app/sitemap.ts` and a sitemap reference in robots.txt
- `app/robots.ts` naming GPTBot, ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, OAI-SearchBot,
  Google-Extended, Bytespider, Amazonbot, CCBot and the rest, each allowed
  explicitly, replacing Cloudflare's default file
- `/llms.txt`, generated by `buildLlmsTxt()` in `answers.ts`, which exists
- Organization, SoftwareApplication, FAQPage and BreadcrumbList JSON-LD
- Canonical tags, one H1 per page, an OG image, unique titles
- Privacy-respecting analytics, because `analytics_missing` fired eight times
  and we cannot measure any of the rest of this plan without it

**Make the proof public.** A page at `/proof` showing our own audit, scored by
our own catalogue, updated on every deploy, with the findings we have not
fixed still listed. Nobody in this category does that. It is the single
cheapest piece of differentiation available and it costs one route.

## Phase 1. Make it buyable

**Target: three to four weeks. This is the phase that decides whether any of
the rest matters.**

Mavek's advantage is not their engine. It is that a founder can give them money
on a Tuesday. Everything below is the checkout page.

### 1.1 A name and a domain

`fullseoaeogeotool.jishhnnuu.workers.dev` cannot be said out loud, cannot be
put on a slide, and will not be cited by an answer engine as a brand because it
is not one. The product already calls itself **SEO OS** in `layout.tsx` and
across the marketing routes. Buy the closest available domain, move the Worker
to it, set `PUBLIC_BASE_URL`, and update both Google OAuth redirect URIs.

Cost: the domain. Time: an afternoon. It blocks brand mentions, which
`mentions.ts` already argues are worth more than links.

### 1.2 Provision the accounts that are already built

`docs/ACCOUNTS.md` describes four browser-only steps: create D1, bind it in
`wrangler.jsonc`, add four secrets, create the Google OAuth client. The code is
written and degrades politely while it is off. Turn it on.

Then start Google verification for the Analytics scope, because it is a
sensitive scope, it takes weeks, and it is on the critical path for the
revenue-reporting claim. Search Console is non-sensitive and needs nothing.
Business Profile needs its own access request; start that too or drop the local
claims from the marketing until it lands.

### 1.3 Payments

There is no billing code in the repository at all. Pick one provider and ship
the smallest version:

- Checkout for Starter and Growth, per site per month, matching the published
  £79 and £249
- A `subscription` table, a webhook route, and plan state on the site record
- Quota enforcement at the crawl boundary: 40 pages free, 250 Starter, 2,000
  Growth, which is where the plans already differ
- A billing screen that can cancel without an email

Use a merchant of record (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy) rather than raw card
processing unless there is a reason not to. A solo operator selling into the EU
and UK should not be handling VAT by hand.

**Do not copy the credit model.** Mavek's credits make cost unforecastable for
the exact buyer they target, and their own pricing page needs eight paragraphs
to explain it. Per site per month is comprehensible on sight, and our marginal
cost is near zero because the audit uses no model and no data vendor.

### 1.4 The schedule that runs without a tab

This is the largest genuine product gap against Mavek and it is the one that
decides whether the word "retainer" is honest.

Today: `schedule.ts` carries `runsHeadless` per job, and only the measurement
sample and the report have it. The audit runs in the browser, so a scheduled
crawl waits for someone to open the app. The product says so on screen, which
is the right handling of a limitation and the wrong thing to still have.

Options, cheapest first:

1. **Run the crawl in the Worker for paying sites.** The fetcher and parser
   already run there. The checks, scoring and fixes are pure functions over a
   crawl report and do not need a DOM. Moving them behind the cron handler is
   mostly plumbing plus a durable queue, because an edge runtime bills CPU per
   request and a 250-page crawl has to be many small invocations.
2. **A small always-on worker** next to the Python stack for Growth and above,
   which already exists as `seoos worker`.

Free stays in the browser. That preserves the property in CLAUDE.md that a
deployment with no database still audits, and it makes the paid tier a real
capability rather than a page-count difference.

### 1.5 The approval queue at scale

Opinion 001 named it: a real site produces 150 findings, the queue asks for 150
decisions, founders make three and close the tab. Mavek does not have this
problem because their queue is campaign-shaped, not finding-shaped.

The fix is batching, and the data to batch on already exists. Every check
carries impact, effort, confidence and `autoFixable`. Group the queue into a
handful of decisions: "apply 38 meta descriptions", "publish the sitemap",
"ship the llms.txt", "these 4 need you to read them". One click each.

A queue of six decisions converts. A queue of 150 does not.

## Phase 2. Close the gaps that lose the deal

### 2.1 Publishing beyond WordPress

WordPress is about 43% of the web, so more than half of prospects currently get
a copy button, which is the exact failure the product exists to fix. The
connector contract already covers Shopify, Webflow, Ghost, Contentful, Sanity,
Strapi and GitHub. Harden the three that cover the most of the remainder and
say plainly on the connections screen which CMSes cannot be written to, with
what the fallback is.

### 2.2 Drafting without the buyer owning a model key

Drafting is the most impressive feature and it is gated behind a key most
founders do not have. The no-key-of-ours rule in CLAUDE.md is load-bearing and
should not be broken for the free tier. The honest version: on paid plans,
drafting runs on our account with the cost priced in and stated, and bring your
own key stays available and cheaper. The free tier keeps working with no key at
all, because the audit and every deterministic fix need none.

### 2.3 Expectation management

Opinion 001: "this will cause more churn than any missing feature." Mavek
promises campaign activity in 48 hours and movement in two to four weeks, which
is credible for paid ads and not credible for organic. We should say the true
thing and make it a differentiator: scores move in days, rankings move in three
to six months, here is what you will see in week one, month one, month three.

`progress.ts` already models six stages with definitions of done and refuses to
score the competitive stage without Search Console. Put that on the marketing
site, not just in the app. It is the most honest onboarding promise in the
category and nobody else can copy it without admitting their own timelines.

## Phase 3. Online presence

**This is where Mavek is beating us 141 pages to 8, and where we have an asset
they cannot match.**

### 3.1 The asymmetry to exploit

Mavek has 19 free tools. They are a word counter, a slug generator, a UTM
builder, a favicon generator. Toys, correctly built as link bait.

We have one free tool that crawls the prospect's real site, runs 90 checks,
writes the fixes, and needs no signup, no card and no key. That is not a
better toy. It is a different category of top-of-funnel asset, and it is
already deployed.

It is currently sitting on a workers.dev subdomain with no distribution behind
it. Phase 1.1 and Phase 3 exist to fix that.

### 3.2 The content programme, in priority order

**Comparison pages first.** They convert at the bottom of the funnel and they
are what answer engines quote when someone asks "what should I use instead of
X". Mavek has ten. We have one, in `docs/`, not on the web. Write:

- vs Search Atlas / OTTO and vs Alli AI. These are the direct rivals and the
  JavaScript-injection argument is devastating against both
- vs Semrush and vs Ahrefs. Research platform against execution platform
- vs Profound and vs Peec. Measurement against work
- vs Mavek. Publish it, concede where they win, the way they concede to
  Profound. It is the most credible page shape in the category
- vs hiring an agency, vs a freelancer

Each one must include the "when you should buy theirs instead" section. Mavek
does this and it is why their comparison pages read as trustworthy.

**Then the wedge essay.** `COMPETITORS.md` contains two facts that decide the
category and are almost unknown outside it:

1. No major AI crawler executes JavaScript. GPTBot downloads it on about 11.5%
   of requests and runs none. ClaudeBot about 23.8% and runs none. Onely's
   February 2026 analysis found 42% of JavaScript-rendered content never
   reaches AI systems.
2. A fix you do not own is a fix you are renting. Search Atlas's own
   documentation confirms schema, meta and redirects revert when the pixel is
   removed.

Together those mean an entire product category sells an AI visibility dashboard
and a script-injected schema fix on the same screen, and the second cannot help
the first. That is a genuine, checkable, uncomfortable argument. It is worth
more than fifty blog posts and it is a natural citation target.

**Then original research, which is the real moat.** We can crawl at scale for
zero marginal cost because the audit uses no model and no data vendor.
Nobody else in this category can, because their cost per site is a model call
or a data credit. So publish the dataset nobody else can afford to produce:

- "We audited the top 1,000 SaaS sites. 31% block at least one AI crawler."
- "How many Series A startups have valid Organization schema."
- "The pixel tools: what actually reaches GPTBot", run against real customer
  sites of the injection tools and checked against their served HTML.
- Refresh quarterly so it becomes a cited series rather than one post.

This does four things at once: it is link bait, it is proof the engine works at
scale, it is the entity-building that `mentions.ts` argues matters more than
links, and it is the kind of primary source an answer engine cites.

**Then the glossary and the programmatic layer.** We have a programmatic SEO
engine and a template-term detector that keeps the keyword model from reading
the navigation back to us. Use our own product on our own site and say so. The
constraint is the one in CLAUDE.md: a listing page with two entries is thin
content that fixes nothing. Build the glossary where there is something to say.

### 3.3 Be citable, not just rankable

Everything in 3.2 should be written to the rules `answers.ts` and the AEO
checks enforce: self-contained passages that state a fact and its context in
one place, question-form headings, explicit entities, citable numbers with
sources. Then run `aeo_tracking` against our own prompt set weekly and publish
the result on `/proof`. Being the AEO tool whose own AEO numbers are public is
a position no competitor can take without exposing themselves.

## Phase 4. Distribution

Content compounds slowly. These do not.

- **Open source is a channel, not just a licence.** Apache-2.0 already. A
  repository that runs a real SEO audit with no keys is a Show HN, a Product
  Hunt launch and a permanent GitHub referral stream. Mavek cannot copy this.
- **Run the tool publicly on famous sites.** A recurring post auditing a
  well-known site with the findings and the written fixes. This is how the
  founder-led credibility in `about` pages is actually built, and our cost per
  audit is zero.
- **Go where the buyer already complains.** r/SEO, r/bigseo, Indie Hackers, the
  SEO corner of X and LinkedIn. The JavaScript-injection argument is a native
  post on all of them.
- **The tool directories.** Get listed where Mavek is: G2, Capterra, and the
  AEO tool roundups that already rank. Several of those roundup pages appeared
  in the research for `COMPARISON-MAVEK.md` and take submissions.
- **Do not build an affiliate programme yet.** Mavek has one. It is premature
  for a product with no retention data.

## Phase 5. Sales

### 5.1 The funnel, in the order it should exist

1. **Free audit, no signup.** Already built, better than anything competing for
   that slot. Add an email capture that is optional and sits after the value,
   not before it. The findings render first, the email asks to save them.
2. **Self-serve Starter and Growth.** No call. This is the volume tier and the
   thing Mavek gates behind an onboarding call, which is their bottleneck.
3. **A paid qualifier for the deal-sized accounts.** Mavek's $9 live audit is a
   good idea for the wrong reason. It works because paying filters for intent,
   not because $9 is revenue. Ours should be the automated audit plus a human
   read of it, and it should scale better than a founder on a call.
4. **Agency and white label.** This is where the revenue actually is, and it is
   already in the pricing as Scale. An agency buying per-site is a multi-site
   account with a single sale, and `COMPETITORS.md` shows nobody serves them
   well at this price point.

### 5.2 The retention mechanic

An agency retainer survives because something arrives every month. Ours is the
report, and `report.ts` already refuses to substitute crawl movement for
business results and says so in the first paragraph when measurement is
missing. That honesty is the retention mechanic: a report that says "flat, and
here is why" is why a client believes the one that says "up 18%".

`milestonesBetween()` emitting only stage completions and regressions is the
other half. A notification for every finding cleared trains people to ignore
notifications.

### 5.3 What not to do

**Do not chase Mavek's scope.** Paid ads, social, CRM and email marketing are
four more products, each with a stronger incumbent, and building them would
dilute the one axis where we win outright. If a prospect wants marketing rather
than search, they are Mavek's customer and we should say so on the comparison
page. That sentence is worth more than the deal.

**Do not copy their marketing failure.** Their site marks four integrations
"coming soon" in one place and live in four others, and ticks a private-beta
AEO tracker on every pricing tier. The moment we tick a feature we have not
shipped, we lose the only asset we have that they cannot buy. Invariant 2 is
not a code rule. It is the brand.

## Progress against this plan

Updated 20 September 2026. This section is appended to rather than rewritten,
so the gap between what was planned and what shipped stays visible.

**Phase 0, done.** The three engine defects are fixed with regression tests.
The site passes its own checks: sitemap, llms.txt, robots naming every search
and AI agent, an entity graph defined once, canonicals, an edge-rendered OG
card. `/proof` runs the audit against this deployment live and publishes the
findings still outstanding. Two new guards gate the build, `brand:check` and
`seo:check`, the second of which exists because fixing the SEO layer
introduced a cascading canonical that pointed eleven pages at the homepage.

**Phase 1, partly done.** Billing exists and is provider-agnostic, quotas are
enforced in the Worker, and the plan definition is single-sourced so the
pricing page cannot drift from enforcement. What remains is external: the
domain, the four billing values, the D1 binding and the Google OAuth client.
`docs/LAUNCH.md` is the checklist.

Two Phase 1 items are untouched and both are real. **The headless crawl** has
not been built, so a scheduled audit still waits for a browser tab and
"retainer" is not yet an honest word. **The approval queue** is still a queue
of 150 decisions rather than six, which Opinion 001 named as the thing
founders bounce off.

**Phase 3, started.** Ten free tools, five comparison pages, sixteen glossary
entries and the JavaScript crawler essay. That is roughly 45 indexable pages
against the competitor's 141, but the tools are a different class of asset:
theirs are word counters, ours run the real catalogue against the visitor's
real page. The original research dataset, which is the actual moat, is not
started.

**Phases 4 and 5, untouched.** No distribution, no launch posts, no
directories, no funnel beyond the free audit itself.

## The one-sentence version

Mavek wins today because they have a checkout page and 141 indexed pages;
we win the engine and we fail our own AEO checks, so the order is: be
believable, be buyable, then be everywhere, in that order and not in parallel.
