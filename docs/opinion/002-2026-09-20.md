# Opinion 002

**20 September 2026.** Written after the competitor teardown, the three engine
defects, the site's own SEO layer, ten free tools, five comparison pages, and
the billing scaffolding.

**One line:** still an exceptional diagnostic, now with a front door and a
checkout, and the honesty discipline is finally applied to our own site rather
than only to other people's. Still not a programme. Do not sell it as an agency
replacement yet.

Opinion 001 stands. Nothing below revises it; this records what changed in four
days and what did not.

---

## What was true at this date

53 agents, 8 missions, 60 tools, 21 connectors, 90 checks in the TypeScript
catalogue and 74 in the Python one, 43 tables, 149 Python tests plus 79 engine
tests. Live on Cloudflare at a workers.dev subdomain, deliberately noindex
until a domain exists. Accounts built but not provisioned. Billing built but
not configured.

## What changed since 001

**Three defects that were real.** `schema_contradicts_page` compared
`description` on any type, so a site-wide `Organization` block marked forty of
forty crawled pages with a high-severity finding. That is the exact failure
invariant 3 exists to prevent, and it shipped. The `measured` flag existed only
in `score.ts`, so the CLI printed "authority 100.0" for a site with no link
data, which is invariant 2 failing on the server side while holding on the
browser side. Both are fixed with regression tests. The flag was being thrown
away at a tool boundary, not at the scorer, which is worth remembering: the
rule was right and the plumbing leaked.

**Our own site failed its own checks.** This is the finding that should sting.
Running our engine against our own deployment returned AEO 51.5 against 62.6
for the competitor we had just written a teardown of. No sitemap, no llms.txt,
no structured data on any page, and Cloudflare's default robots file
containing thirty lines of comments and not one directive. We had been
auditing other people for a week.

That is now fixed, and `/proof` publishes the result permanently, including
the findings still outstanding. Nobody else in this category shows their own
score. It is the cheapest differentiation available and it cost one route.

**A bug I introduced while fixing that.** Setting `alternates.canonical` in the
root layout cascades, so eleven pages declared the homepage as their canonical.
High severity, worse than a missing canonical, and it was caught only by
reading the rendered values rather than checking the tag existed. `check-seo.mjs`
now gates the build. Presence-checking would have passed.

**A front door.** Ten free tools, five comparison pages, sixteen glossary
entries and the JavaScript crawler essay. The tools are the asymmetry worth
having: everyone else's free tools are word counters and slug generators built
as link bait, and ours run the real catalogue against the visitor's real page.
That is affordable to give away only because the audit uses no model and no
data vendor, which is the same property that makes the whole product cheap to
operate.

**A checkout.** One plan definition that the pricing page derives from and the
quota check enforces, so the two cannot disagree. Provider-agnostic behind
three functions. Nothing configured, so nothing is charged, but the gap between
"has no billing code" and "has billing code waiting for four values" is the
gap between a project and a product.

**A launch that is two variables.** Everything reads from `lib/brand.ts`, and
`check-brand.mjs` fails the build if a literal creeps back in.

## What has not changed at all

Every one of Opinion 001's six blind spots except the last two is still open,
and the two that closed did so in the commits just before 001 was written
rather than since.

1. **No expectation management on the marketing site.** `progress.ts` models it
   in the app. The public pages still do not say "scores move in days,
   rankings move in three to six months" anywhere a prospect reads before
   signing up. This remains the thing most likely to cause churn.
2. **No competitive content analysis.** Rivals are still compared on schema and
   crawler access, not on whether their page answers the query better.
3. **No internal linking execution.** Orphans are identified; building the
   listing page that fixes twenty-eight of them is still manual.
4. **Local SEO is drafts only.**
5. **The audit still needs a browser tab.** This is the big one. The schedule
   runs the measurement sample and the report headless; a scheduled crawl waits
   for someone to open the app. Until that changes, "retainer" is not an honest
   word for what this sells, and the product says so on screen, which is the
   right handling of the wrong situation.
6. **The approval queue is still a queue of 150 decisions.** Founders make
   three and close the tab. Every check carries impact, effort, confidence and
   `autoFixable`, so the data to collapse it into six decisions exists and is
   unused.

## What I would say to a buyer today

The same as four days ago, with one addition.

Do not sell "replaces your SEO agency". Sell "the audit and the fixes an agency
charges two thousand pounds for, in ten minutes, free", which is now
demonstrably true because ten of those checks run as their own tools with no
signup at all.

The addition: we can now also say "and we publish our own score", and mean it.
That is a smaller claim than an agency replacement and a much harder one for a
better-funded competitor to copy, because copying it means showing their own
number.

## The blunt version

Opinion 001 said 30% of an agency's value is knowing what is wrong and 70% is
doing it every week for a year, and that the 30% was nailed while the 70% was
started. That is still the position. What changed is that the 30% now has a
shopfront, a price, and a page proving we hold ourselves to it.

A shopfront is not a programme. The next honest milestone is the headless
crawl, because that is the one that turns a tool someone opens into a service
that turns up on Tuesday.
